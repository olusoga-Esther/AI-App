# **STEM BRIDGE**

## **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

### **1\. Product Overview**

**STEM Bridge** is a digital platform designed to make STEM exploration accessible to children who may not have access to expensive STEM kits, laboratories, computers, or formal STEM programs.

The platform provides simple STEM activities and challenges that children can explore using everyday materials, free resources, and basic digital devices.

**Core idea:**  
Every child should have an opportunity to explore STEM.

---

# **2\. Product Goal**

The goal of STEM Bridge is to help children discover and explore STEM through activities that are:

* Accessible  
* Simple to understand  
* Age-appropriate  
* Practical  
* Engaging  
* Low-cost or free

The product should reduce the resource barrier that can prevent children from experiencing STEM.

---

# **3\. Target Users**

### **Primary Users**

Children aged **6–12**.

### **Supporting Users**

Parents, teachers, and caregivers who may help children access and complete activities.

---

# **4\. Problem Statement**

Many children are interested in STEM but do not have equal access to STEM learning opportunities.

Some children have access to robotics kits, laboratories, computers, coding programs, and STEM clubs, while others may have limited resources.

STEM Bridge provides another starting point: children can begin exploring STEM with what they already have.

---

# **5\. Product Solution**

STEM Bridge provides a library of STEM experiences organised into:

* Science  
* Technology  
* Engineering  
* Mathematics

Children can choose an area, select an activity or challenge, follow simple instructions, and reflect on what they discover.

Activities should prioritise everyday materials and should clearly show what resources are required before the child begins.

---

# **6\. Core User Journey**

The primary journey is:

**Open STEM Bridge → Choose STEM Area → Choose Activity → Learn → Try → Think → Create**

### **Step 1: Explore**

The child chooses a STEM area that interests them.

### **Step 2: Learn**

The platform introduces the concept using simple, child-friendly language.

### **Step 3: Try**

The child completes an experiment, activity, or challenge.

### **Step 4: Think**

The child answers questions, makes observations, or explains what happened.

### **Step 5: Create**

The child modifies, designs, builds, or creates something based on what they learned.

---

# **7\. MVP Features**

## **7.1 Home**

The home screen should immediately communicate what STEM Bridge does.

It should include:

**STEM Bridge**

*"Explore. Experiment. Create."*

A short introduction and access to the STEM categories.

---

## **7.2 STEM Categories**

Children can choose from:

### **🔬 Science**

Activities involving observation, experiments, nature, materials, forces, light, sound, and other scientific concepts.

### **💻 Technology**

Activities involving algorithms, computational thinking, logic, sequencing, coding, and digital exploration.

### **⚙️ Engineering**

Activities involving designing, building, testing, problem-solving, and improving solutions.

### **🔢 Mathematics**

Activities involving numbers, patterns, shapes, measurement, logic, data, and problem-solving.

---

# **8\. Activity Requirements**

Every activity should contain the following:

### **Activity Title**

A simple and engaging name.

### **Age Range**

The recommended age.

### **Time**

Estimated completion time.

### **Materials**

A clear list of what is needed.

### **Big Question**

The main question the child is exploring.

### **Activity**

Simple step-by-step instructions.

### **Challenge**

A problem for the child to solve or investigate.

### **Think About It**

Questions that encourage observation and reasoning.

### **Create or Improve**

An opportunity for the child to change, build, or improve their solution.

### **What Did You Discover?**

A short reflection section.

### **STEM Skills**

The skills or concepts explored.

---

# **9\. Activity Types**

STEM Bridge should include different ways of learning.

### **Explore**

Investigate something in the child's environment.

Example:

**Magnet Hunt**

Find objects around you and discover which ones are attracted to a magnet.

### **Build**

Create something using available materials.

Example:

**Paper Bridge**

Build a bridge using paper and test how much weight it can hold.

### **Solve**

Find a solution to a problem.

Example:

**Build a Shelter**

Design a shelter that protects an object from water.

### **Predict**

Make a prediction and test it.

Example:

**Sink or Float**

Predict which objects will sink before testing them.

### **Think**

Solve a logic or computational-thinking problem.

Example:

**Be the Robot**

Give another person precise instructions to reach a destination.

### **Create**

Design something independently.

Example:

**Design Your Own Paper Airplane**

---

# **10\. Accessibility Requirements**

Accessibility is a central part of STEM Bridge.

Activities should be divided according to the resources required.

### **No Equipment**

Activities that require only thinking, observation, movement, or communication.

### **Everyday Materials**

Activities using items such as:

Paper

Cups

Water

Cardboard

String

Pencils

Household objects

### **Basic Digital Device**

Activities that require a phone, tablet, or computer.

The platform should make the required resources visible before the child begins an activity.

The first version should contain:

### **1\. Home**

Introduction and STEM categories.

### **2\. Explore**

List of STEM activities and challenges.

### **3\. Activity Details**

Complete instructions and learning information.

### **4\. Challenge**

### **5\. Saved Activities**

A place to keep activities the child wants to try later.

---

# **13\. Functional Requirements**

The system should allow users to:

1. Browse STEM categories.  
2. View available activities.  
3. Filter activities by age or resource requirements.  
4. Open an activity.  
5. View materials and instructions.  
6. Complete activity challenges.  
7. View reflection questions.  
8. Save activities for later.  
9. Return to previously saved activities.

---

# **14\. Non-Functional Requirements**

The platform should be:

**Simple:** Children should understand how to navigate it.

**Responsive:** It should work on phones, tablets, and computers.

**Accessible:** Activities should not depend primarily on expensive resources.

**Safe:** Activities should include appropriate safety guidance where necessary.

**Child-friendly:** Language and instructions should be appropriate for the target age group.

---

# **15\. MVP Success Criteria**

The MVP is successful if a child can:

**Choose a STEM area → Find an activity → Understand what is needed → Complete the activity → Think about what happened → Try another activity.**

The product should demonstrate that children can meaningfully explore STEM without requiring access to specialised STEM equipment.

---

# **16\. Future Possibilities**

Future versions may include:

* Child profiles  
* Parent accounts  
* Teacher accounts  
* Progress tracking  
* STEM learning paths  
* Badges and achievements  
* Personalised activity recommendations  
* Printable activity resources  
* Interactive simulations  
* Coding environments  
* Community challenges  
* Offline access

These features are outside the MVP and should only be added after the core experience has been tested.

---

# **17\. Product Principle**

STEM Bridge is built around one central principle:

> **A child's access to STEM exploration should not be limited by the resources they have.**

The product should help children move from **curiosity to exploration, exploration to problem-solving, and problem-solving to creation.**

**STEM Bridge — Explore. Experiment. Create.**
